Client project

Barbershop website and online booking

My role
Sole developer and consultant: research, pitch, planning and build.
Problem
A two-chair barbershop in Bradford had no website and took every booking by phone and text. That meant missed calls, double bookings and no reminders, and the shop was almost invisible on Google.

Module case study

Ember & Oak: restaurant digital transformation

My role
Acting CTO of a fictional Salford grill house. I planned, designed and hand-coded the solution.
Problem
The restaurant takes bookings by phone, its menu is a PDF that's hard to read on mobile, it has no way to collect feedback, and its promotions only reach people who walk past.
Solution
A single, mobile-first Problem-Solving Page with a filterable menu, a live "open now" status, a booking and feedback form that writes the email for the customer, a loyalty sign-up, social links and a clear privacy notice.
Outcome
A working prototype that shows how simple web technology can cut phone time for staff, make booking easier for customers and give the owner direct feedback.
Skills used
HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript, form validation, accessibility, GDPR awareness, basic SEO, GitHub and GitHub Pages.

Why I structured the page this way

  1. Business context first. The page opens with the restaurant's goals, so every feature after it has a clear reason to be there.
  2. Menu before the form. Customers usually check the food before they book, so the menu comes before the booking form. The filter buttons let someone with dietary needs find their options in one tap.
  3. One form, three jobs. Booking, feedback and loyalty sign-up share one form, and the fields change depending on the choice. That keeps the page short and needs no database: the form writes a ready-to-send email instead.
  4. Contact and social at the end. People who don't want to book online can still find the phone number, address and hours, and promotions can be shared on Facebook, Instagram and X.
  5. Privacy built in. The form asks only for what the restaurant needs, explains how the data is used and needs a consent tick, in line with UK GDPR.
  6. Mobile and accessibility. The layout is built for phones first, uses real labels on every field, has strong colour contrast and works with a keyboard.
