Client project

Barbershop website and online booking

My role
Sole developer and consultant: research, pitch, planning and build.
Problem
A two-chair barbershop in Bradford had no website and took every booking by phone and text. That meant missed calls, double bookings and no reminders, and the shop was almost invisible on Google.
Solution
A mobile-first website with services, prices, opening hours and a map, plus an online booking system with live availability, text confirmations and reminders.
Outcome
I went into a barbershop in Bradford and pitched a new website with an automated booking system. To set my price, I looked at what other people were charging for similar work and weighed that up against my own situation. The owner said yes. The biggest thing I learned was to build around the problems the shop actually deals with day to day, not just what looks good.
Skills used
Business research, requirements gathering, pricing, responsive web design, booking-system integration.
Own business

Web design studio and client outreach

My role
Founder: branding, website, copywriting and outreach.
Problem
Many local salons and clinics had outdated websites or none at all, and were losing customers to competitors who were easier to find and book online.
Solution
I set up a small web design studio with its own website and logo, researched local businesses, and ran personalised email campaigns in batches with planned follow-ups.
Outcome
I sent 27 personalised emails in four batches. I got a few replies, but none of them turned into a project. It still taught me a lot about researching a market, writing for the person reading it, and keeping track of what was and wasn’t working.
Skills used
Market research, digital marketing, copywriting, web design, organising a campaign.

Module case study

Ember & Oak: restaurant digital transformation

My role
Acting CTO of a fictional Salford grill house. I planned and designed the solution, used an AI tool to help draft the code (declared in my GenAI statement), then rewrote, adapted and tested it myself.
Problem
The restaurant takes bookings by phone, its menu is a PDF that’s hard to read on mobile, it has no way to collect feedback, and its promotions only reach people who walk past.
Solution
A single, mobile-first Problem-Solving Page with a filterable menu, a live "open now" status, a steak cooking guide, a booking and feedback form with an allergies box that writes the email for the customer, a loyalty sign-up, social links and a clear privacy notice.
Outcome
A working prototype that shows how a simple website could take pressure off staff answering the phone, make booking easier for customers, and give the owner feedback straight from the people eating there.
Skills used
HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript, form validation, accessibility, GDPR awareness, basic SEO, GitHub and GitHub Pages.

Why I structured the page this way

  1. Business first. I started the page with what the restaurant is trying to achieve, so every feature after that has a clear reason to be there.
  2. Menu before booking. When I was waiting tables, most people wanted to see the food before they committed to anything, so the menu comes before the booking form. At Hot Rock the menu was a QR code that was awkward to read on your phone, so I made sure this one is easy to read on a small screen.
  3. One form, three jobs. Booking, feedback and loyalty sign-up all use the same form, so customers aren’t filling in three different things. There’s no database. The form just creates an email that’s ready to send, which keeps it cheap and simple for a small restaurant.
  4. Contact at the end. Not everyone wants to book online. Loads of customers I served would rather just ring up, so the phone number, address and opening hours are easy to find at the bottom.
  5. Privacy built in. The form only asks for what the restaurant actually needs, explains how the data will be used, and needs a consent tick. That follows the data-minimisation idea in UK GDPR.
  6. Phones first and accessible. Most people look up restaurants on their phone, often when they’re already out, so I designed for phones first. Every field has a proper label, the colours have strong contrast, and the whole page works with just a keyboard.
